About

Twitter LinkedIn Github Mastodon Hugging Face

I spent around fifteen years as a historian and researcher, mostly in and about Afghanistan, before moving into software in 2021. Since then I’ve been an AI/ML engineer at ZenML, working on ZenML and Kitaru. I live in Delft, in the Netherlands, with two cats.

Most of my historical work came down to reading large piles of messy, contradictory sources, deciding which ones to trust, and turning them into something structured enough to reason about. That turns out to be most of the work of evaluating and training agents. Reading an agent’s trace is source criticism: its account of what it did is testimony, not fact. Building an eval or an RL environment means turning a messy record into a task you can check, which is what I did with ISAF press releases on the way to finetuned models that beat GPT-4. I’m always happy to talk about evals, RL environments or agent observability; the easiest way to reach me is on LinkedIn or Twitter.

This site has two halves. The technical side is where I work things out in public: these days that’s mostly agents, reinforcement learning for agents, and evals. The personal side goes back to 2010 and covers Afghanistan, language learning, maths, learning how to learn, and the books I’ve been reading. If you’re not sure where to start, the series collect longer runs of posts about a single project, course or book. Each half has its own RSS feed (technical, personal).

The two halves meet more often than you might expect. Some of the tools below are for historians and researchers, and I publish datasets on Hugging Face, including ISAF press releases from Afghanistan and records from the Guantánamo military commissions.
